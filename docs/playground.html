Market making on Apple's order book, with and without an order-flow filter

Adam Nguyen|University of Washington, Mathematics and Informatics|A playable working paper

Abstract.A market maker quotes Apple on a day of real Nasdaq order flow, rebuilt message by message from TotalView-ITCH with queue position tracked exactly. A gradient-boosted model of the next mid-price move reaches a test AUC of 0.652, against 0.603 for the standard book-imbalance signal, and is used to pull quotes it expects to be run over. With every threshold and activity rate chosen on a tuning hour before the test afternoon is run, the filter cuts the afternoon's loss from −$1,147 to −$470 at zero latency, and it loses less per share than imbalance at matched activity. With latency added, the best ML filter still cuts the loss by more than half at every setting up to 10 ms, and on a second, untouched day the fill-toxicity model holds up best. Every run below is real and precomputed; change the settings in Figure 1 to load any of them.

5Method and limits

Filters. Next move predicts the direction of the next mid-price change. Latency-trained predicts the first change after a cancel could land, since nothing can dodge a move inside that gap. Toxicity predicts the 1-second markout of a fill on each side, trained on the fills the plain market maker got in the morning. Imbalance is the baseline: how hard the best bid and ask lean one way.

Split. Train 9:35–12:30, tune 12:30–1:30, test 1:30–3:55, with a 60-second gap at each boundary and no shuffling. Thresholds, activity rates and model settings all come from the tuning hour; the test afternoon is run once.

Backtest. Quotes are shadow orders whose queue position is tracked exactly from Nasdaq's order-level feed, with place and cancel latency and a $0.0020 per share rebate.

Fair comparison. Each filter's thresholds are set so it fires the same share of the time on the tuning hour, so loss per share is compared at equal activity.

Limits.

  • Two days of one stock for the backtests, the second held out entirely. Better than one, not a track record.
  • Shadow orders have no market impact, so fill counts run high.
  • The rebate is assumed; auction and hidden-order fills are not modelled.
  • The tuning hour is short, so the rate it picks is a noisy choice. That is the cost of not peeking.

A note on an earlier result. An earlier version of this page reported a profit of +$160 at zero latency. That activity rate had been chosen by looking at the test afternoon. Chosen on the tuning hour instead, as everything here now is, the result is the −$470 above.
