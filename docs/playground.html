How this was built

Model. Gradient-boosted trees predict whether AAPL's next mid-price move is up or down, from order-flow features computed only from events up to that moment: depth imbalance at 1, 2 and 5 levels, order-flow imbalance over 100 ms, 1 s and 10 s, signed trade volume, recent returns and activity. One sample per timestamp, taken after a whole sweep has been applied.

Honest split. Train 9:35–12:30, tune 12:30–13:30, test 13:30–15:55 (including the 2 pm Fed announcement), with a 60 s embargo at each boundary. No shuffling. The test period is used once, and everything on this page comes from it.

Backtest. Quotes are shadow orders whose queue position is tracked exactly from Nasdaq's order-level feed, with place/cancel latency and a $0.0020/share rebate. The filter pulls the ask while the model expects an up-move (and the bid for a down-move).

Fair comparison. The imbalance and model thresholds are set so each filter is active the same share of the time on the tuning period. Pulling more always means trading less, so the right question is loss per share at equal pull rates.

Limits

  • One stock, one day, one test afternoon. The AUC intervals (block bootstrap over 1-minute blocks) cover sampling noise inside that afternoon, not other days.
  • Shadow orders have no market impact, so fill counts are on the high side.
  • The rebate is assumed; auction and hidden-order fills are not modelled.
  • The model is scored on the next move's direction, not its timing. A correct prediction can still arrive after the quote is hit.
