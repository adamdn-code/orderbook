How this was built

Models. Gradient-boosted trees on 19 order-flow features computed only from events up to that moment (depth imbalance at 1, 2 and 5 levels, order-flow imbalance over 100 ms, 1 s and 10 s, signed trade volume, queue sizes, recent moves). Next move predicts the direction of the next mid-price change. Latency-trained predicts the first change after the cancel could land, since nothing can dodge a move inside that gap. Fill toxicity predicts the 1-second markout of a fill on each side, trained directly on the fills the plain market maker got in the morning.

Honest split. Train 9:35–12:30, tune 12:30–13:30, test 13:30–15:55 (including the 2 pm Fed announcement), 60 s embargo, never shuffled. Thresholds, pull rates and model settings all come from the tuning hour. The test period is used once.

Backtest. Quotes are shadow orders whose queue position is tracked exactly from Nasdaq's order-level feed, with place/cancel latency and a $0.0020/share rebate. A triggered filter either pulls the threatened quote or steps it back a cent.

Fair comparison. Each filter's thresholds are set so it is active the same share of the time on the tuning hour. Pulling more always means trading less, so the question is loss per share at equal activity.

Limits

  • The backtests are one stock's afternoon. The second day and the other stocks test the model's predictions more broadly; see the panels above for how far they go.
  • Shadow orders have no market impact, so fill counts are on the high side.
  • The rebate is assumed; auction and hidden-order fills are not modelled.
  • The tuning hour is short, so the pull rate it picks is a noisy choice. That's the honest cost of not peeking.
