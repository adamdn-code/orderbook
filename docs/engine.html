The matching engine, running in your browser

This is the C++ limit order book from the repository, cpp/src/orderbook.cpp unmodified, compiled to a 16 KB WebAssembly module. It starts from Apple's real top-of-book at 1:59:49 pm on 30 January 2019, eleven seconds before the Fed statement. Place an order, watch where it joins the queue, and turn on other traders to see it fill.

    "Other traders" is random flow, not history: about a dozen events a second of limit orders near the touch, cancels, and orders that take liquidity. It exists to move the book so your orders can fill.

    How fast is it here?

    The same benchmark the C++ suite runs natively: random limit orders within ten cents of the mid, with 30% cancels, on a separate book so yours is untouched.

    Natively the book handles 6.1M orders/s on one core of a 2-core cloud VM, with a 99th-percentile latency of 352 ns. The WebAssembly build matches the native one trade for trade: one million orders give 557,474 trades in both. How the engine works · the WebAssembly build.
